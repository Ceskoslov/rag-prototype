# Regulatory Compliance Overview (FAA, EASA)

## Overview
SkyWing Airlines (SkyWing) is a global airline and must comply with the aviation regulations of the countries in which it operates. This document provides an overview of the key regulatory compliance requirements for SkyWing, specifically addressing the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) in the United States and the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) in the European Union.

## Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)
The FAA is the primary aviation regulatory body in the United States and is responsible for ensuring the safety and efficiency of civil aviation. SkyWing must comply with the FAA's regulations, including:
- **Title 14 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR)**: This title contains the FAA's regulations for aviation, including Part 121 (Operating Requirements: Domestic, Flag, and Supplemental Operations) and Part 145 (Repair Stations).
- **Airworthiness Directives (ADs)**: The FAA issues ADs to address unsafe conditions on aircraft, engines, propellers, or appliances. SkyWing must comply with all applicable ADs.
- **Maintenance and Inspection Programs**: SkyWing must maintain its aircraft in accordance with FAA-approved maintenance and inspection programs.
- **Flight Operations and Crew Training**: SkyWing must ensure that its flight operations and crew training meet FAA standards.
- **Drug and Alcohol Testing**: SkyWing must comply with the FAA's drug and alcohol testing requirements for employees in safety-sensitive positions.

## European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA)
EASA is the primary aviation regulatory body in the European Union and is responsible for ensuring the safety and environmental protection of civil aviation. SkyWing must comply with EASA's regulations, including:
- **Regulation (EU) 2018/1139**: This regulation (the "Basic Regulation") provides the legal framework for aviation safety in the EU.
- **Part-CAT (Commercial Air Transport Operations)**: This part of the EASA regulations sets out the requirements for commercial air transport operations.
- **Part-M (Continuing Airworthiness Requirements)**: This part of the EASA regulations sets out the requirements for the continuing airworthiness of aircraft and aeronautical products.
- **Part-145 (Maintenance Organization Approval)**: This part of the EASA regulations sets out the requirements for maintenance organizations.
- **Licensing and Training**: SkyWing must ensure that its pilots and other crew members are licensed and trained in accordance with EASA standards.

## International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO)
SkyWing also complies with the standards and recommended practices (SARPs) of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), a specialized agency of the United Nations. ICAO's SARPs cover various aspects of international civil aviation, including:
- Personnel licensing.
- Rules of the air.
- Meteorological services.
- Aeronautical charts.
- Units of measurement.
- Operation of aircraft.
- Aircraft nationality and registration marks.
- Airworthiness of aircraft.
- Facilitation (customs and immigration).
- Aeronautical telecommunications.
- Air traffic services.
- Search and rescue.
- Aircraft accident and incident investigation.
- Aerodromes.
- Aeronautical information services.
- Environmental protection.
- Security.
- Safe transport of dangerous goods.

## Compliance Monitoring and Auditing
SkyWing has a robust compliance monitoring and auditing program to ensure that it meets all applicable regulatory requirements.
- **Safety Management System (SMS)**: SkyWing has implemented an SMS to identify and manage safety risks.
- **Internal Audits**: SkyWing's Internal Audit department conducts regular audits of its operations, maintenance, and training programs to ensure compliance with FAA, EASA, and other regulatory standards.
- **Regulatory Inspections**: SkyWing is subject to regular inspections by the FAA, EASA, and other national aviation authorities.

## Reporting Non-Compliance
Employees and partners must report any suspected or actual non-compliance with aviation regulations.
- **SkyWing Safety Reporting System**: Reports can be made through SkyWing's internal safety reporting system (e.g., ASAP, VDRP).
- **SkyWing Legal Department**: Reports can also be made to the Legal Department at `legal-compliance@skywing.com`.

## Disciplinary Action and Legal Consequences
Failure to comply with aviation regulations may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment. SkyWing and its employees may also face legal consequences, including fines, penalties, and the suspension or revocation of licenses and operating certificates.
