# Passenger Rights and Compensation Policy (EU261, DOT)

## Overview
SkyWing Airlines is committed to providing a high standard of service and respecting the rights of our passengers in accordance with international and regional regulations. This document outlines SkyWing's policies for passenger rights and compensation, specifically focusing on EU Regulation 261/2004 and US Department of Transportation (DOT) mandates.

## EU Regulation 261/2004 (EU261)
EU261 applies to all SkyWing flights departing from an airport within the European Union (EU), European Economic Area (EEA), or Switzerland, as well as SkyWing flights arriving in the EU/EEA/Switzerland when operated by a Community carrier.

### Flight Cancellations
If a flight is cancelled, passengers are entitled to:
- **Right to Care**: Two free phone calls, emails, or faxes; food and refreshments in reasonable relation to the waiting time; hotel accommodation and transport between the airport and the place of accommodation (if an overnight stay is required).
- **Choice of Re-routing or Refund**: A full refund of the ticket price within 7 days, or re-routing to the final destination at the earliest opportunity or at a later date at the passenger's convenience (subject to seat availability).
- **Fixed-Sum Compensation**: Unless informed of the cancellation at least 14 days before the scheduled departure time, or if the cancellation is caused by "extraordinary circumstances" (e.g., extreme weather, air traffic control strikes, security threats).

#### Compensation Amounts (EU261)
- **EUR 250**: For all flights of 1,500km or less.
- **EUR 400**: For all intra-Community flights of more than 1,500km, and for all other flights between 1,500km and 3,500km.
- **EUR 600**: For all flights not falling under the above (over 3,500km).
*Note: Compensation may be reduced by 50% if the arrival time of the re-routed flight does not exceed the scheduled arrival time of the original flight by 2, 3, or 4 hours, respectively.*

### Flight Delays
Passengers are entitled to the "Right to Care" (meals, refreshments, and communication) if their flight is delayed:
- **2 hours or more**: For flights of 1,500km or less.
- **3 hours or more**: For intra-Community flights of more than 1,500km, and for all other flights between 1,500km and 3,500km.
- **4 hours or more**: For all other flights.
*If the delay is 5 hours or more, passengers have the right to a refund of their ticket.*

### Denied Boarding (Bumping)
If SkyWing is unable to provide a seat on a flight for which a passenger has a confirmed reservation, we will first call for volunteers to surrender their seats in exchange for benefits. If an insufficient number of volunteers come forward and a passenger is denied boarding against their will, they are entitled to:
- **Immediate Fixed-Sum Compensation**: As per the amounts specified for flight cancellations.
- **Right to Choice**: Re-routing or refund.
- **Right to Care**.

## US Department of Transportation (DOT) Regulations
For flights departing from or arriving at a US airport, SkyWing adheres to the DOT's consumer protection rules.

### Tarmac Delay Rule
SkyWing will not permit an aircraft to remain on the tarmac at a US airport for more than:
- **3 hours**: For domestic flights.
- **4 hours**: For international flights.
*Exceptions apply for safety, security, and air traffic control reasons. After 2 hours, SkyWing must provide passengers with food, water, operable lavatories, and medical attention if needed.*

### Denied Boarding Compensation (DBC)
For "oversold" flights departing from a US airport:
- **Volunteers**: SkyWing will offer compensation (vouchers or cash) and re-booking.
- **Involuntary Denied Boarding**: Compensation is based on the arrival time at the final destination compared to the original scheduled arrival:
    - **0-1 hour delay**: No compensation.
    - **1-4 hour delay**: 200% of the one-way fare (up to $775).
    - **Over 4 hour delay**: 400% of the one-way fare (up to $1,550).

## Extraordinary Circumstances
SkyWing is not required to pay fixed-sum compensation under EU261 if the disruption was caused by "extraordinary circumstances" that could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken. These include:
- Extreme weather conditions (e.g., hurricanes, volcanic ash).
- Air Traffic Control (ATC) strikes or restrictions.
- Political instability or security threats.
- Unforeseen flight safety shortcomings (e.g., bird strikes, lightning strikes).
- *Technical faults discovered during routine maintenance are generally NOT considered extraordinary.*

## Claims Process
Passengers must submit claims for compensation in writing via the SkyWing online claims portal at `legal.skywing.com/claims`. Claims must include:
- Passenger name and contact information.
- Booking reference (PNR).
- Flight number and date.
- Description of the disruption.
SkyWing will respond to all claims within 30 days of receipt.

## Escalation
If a passenger is unsatisfied with SkyWing's response, they may escalate their complaint to the relevant National Enforcement Body (NEB) in the EU or the Aviation Consumer Protection Division (ACPD) in the US.
